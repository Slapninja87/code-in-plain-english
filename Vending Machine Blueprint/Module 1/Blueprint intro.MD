Before a developer writes a single line of code, they have to break a problem down into tiny, unmistakable steps. Computers are incredibly fast, but they are also incredibly literal. They cannot fill in the blanks or guess what you mean. 
To see what we mean, let's look at a machine everyone has used: A Vending Machine. 

The Plain English Logic
Imagine you are building the brain for a vending machine. Your goal is to check if a person has enough money to buy a soda that costs $1.50. 
If you were talking to a human, you'd just say, "Check if they have enough money for a soda." A human brain automatically does the math. But for a computer, you have to write out the recipe step-by-step: 
•	Step 1 (Memory): Remember that a soda costs exactly $1.50. 
•	Step 2 (The Question): Ask the user, "How much money are you putting in?" 
•	Step 3 (The Input): Look at the physical cash or coins they hand over and count it. 
•	Step 4 (The Decision): 
	o	IF the money they gave you is greater than or equal to $1.50, dispense the soda and say "Enjoy your drink!" 
	o	ELSE (if the money is less than $1.50), keep the door locked and say "Inadequate funds." 

			[ Is the money >= $1.50? ]
			                 /          \
			              YES            NO
			              /                \
			   "Enjoy your drink!"   "Inadequate funds."
